108 Hanuman Temple in Nepal
Jagadguru Mahayogi Siddhababa has undertaken a sacred Sat-Sankalpa (holy vow) to establish 108 Hanuman Temples across Nepal—a visionary initiative dedicated to spiritual renewal, cultural preservation, and social upliftment.
These temples are envisioned not only as places of worship, but as vibrant community centers rooted in Sanatan Dharma. Built with the support of local communities and managed by local committees, each temple will serve as a sustainable hub for spiritual education and collective growth.
Every temple complex will include:
A multi-purpose hall accommodating approximately 200–250 people
Dedicated rooms to support daily worship and temple activities
Spaces designed for education, community engagement, and sadhana
Each location will function as a Mahayog Meditation Center, offering:
Teachings on Sanatan Dharma and Hindu philosophy
Yogic and Vedic science education
Meditation and Himalayan Siddha Mahayog practice
Youth character development initiatives
Cultural education rooted in sanskar and sanskriti
Regular satsang and group sadhana programs
“The body is temporary; it will pass. Let us leave behind something of lasting value that teaches sanskar and sanskriti to future generations.” – Jagadguru Mahayogi Siddhababa
Sacred Infrastructure & Community Design
Hanumanji Murti & Daily Astayaam Sewa
Each temple will feature a majestic murti (sacred statue) of Lord Hanuman and daily Astayaam Sewa will ensure a continuous flow of worship, remembrance, and spiritual energy within the sacred space.
Temple Facilities
Kitchen (Anna Seva Area)
A space for preparing and sharing prasad, strengthening community bonds through nourishment and seva.
Pujari Room
A designated area for ritual preparation and daily worship activities.
Store Room
For safekeeping sacred items and materials required for ceremonies.
Administrative Office
To coordinate programs, maintain records, and ensure smooth temple operations.
Multi-purpose Hall
The heart of the temple complex—adaptable for:
Satsang and spiritual discourses
Group meditation and Mahayog sadhana
Cultural celebrations and festivals
Youth programs and community gatherings
This hall is designed to be flexible and inclusive, fostering connection and collective spiritual growth.
Purpose of the Initiative
The primary aim of this national mission is to reintroduce the profound teachings of Sanatan Dharma in a scientific, practical, and experiential manner.
Through daily group sadhana, structured satsang classes, and community-centered activities, these temples seek to cultivate:
Spiritual awareness
Moral and cultural strength
Intergenerational transmission of wisdom
Social harmony rooted in dharmic values
This initiative represents a long-term investment in preserving and revitalizing Nepal’s spiritual and cultural heritage.

Ram Mandir (Dev Sabha) 
A historic Ram Mandir will be built in Jagadguru Ramanadacharya Seva Peeth (Tarak Brahma Peeth), Chatara Dham. It’s envisioned as a spiritually and architecturally extraordinary temple—designed as a sacred replica of Dev Sabha, the divine assembly of the 33 koti devatas, with Lord Ram, Maa Sita and family at the center.
This temple will not only be a place of worship, but a living spiritual and cosmological experience. Its architecture will be based on the structure of the universe as described in Vedic scriptures and realized through the spiritual insights of Jagadguru Mahayogi Siddhababa.
Visitors will journey through the temple as they would journey through creation itself.
A Temple Designed as the Universe
The Ram Mandir is conceived as a spiritual map of cosmic origin and human evolution.
After entering the temple, visitors will experience a guided progression through halls that symbolically represent:
The state of primordial nothingness
The emergence of the three fundamental qualities (Tri-Guna: Sattva, Rajas, Tamas)
The manifestation of Prakriti (primordial nature)
The evolution into Mahat (cosmic intelligence)
The formation of Ahankar (individual identity/ego)
The unfolding of creation into the present age
The temple will also present teachings on the four Yugas:
Satya Yuga
Treta Yuga
Dwapar Yuga
Kali Yuga
Through this immersive experience, visitors will gain insight into Vedic cosmology, the cycle of time, and dharmic living across ages.

Architectural Highlights
Nine-Storey Temple Structure
A majestic nine-storey temple symbolizing ascent through layers of consciousness and existence.
Sri Yantra–Based Architecture
The temple design will be based on sacred geometry, particularly the Sri Yantra, representing the union of cosmic energies and the structure of creation itself.
33 Koti Devatas
The temple will symbolically house the 33 koti devatas, arranged according to universal Vastu principles described in Vedic scriptures, with Lord Ram and His divine family at the spiritual center.
Cultural & Historical Integration
Representation of Nepal’s Cultural Diversity
The temple will serve as a living model of Nepal’s rich cultural and traditional heritage, integrating elements from diverse communities across the nation.
Display of Historical Nepal
Within its sacred halls, visitors will encounter curated representations of Nepal’s historical, spiritual, and cultural evolution—offering both devotional inspiration and educational value.

A Spiritual and Educational Landmark
The Ram Mandir at Chatara Dham is envisioned as:
A center for devotion
A hub for Vedic knowledge
A cultural preservation initiative
A sacred architectural landmark
A spiritual pilgrimage destination
It will stand as a symbol of harmony between science, spirituality, architecture, and cultural identity.
