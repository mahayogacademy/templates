Jadguru Mahayogi Siddhababa 
Anant Shri Vibhushit Jagadguru Ramanandacharya Swami Ramakrishnacharya Ji Maharaj is popularly known as Jadaguru Mahayogi Siddhababa and “Gurudev” by his students. He is a realized saint in the Ramanandi (Sri Vaishnava) tradition. His Holiness is a Tridandi sannyasi renowned for his mastery of scriptures, yoga and Kundalini science. 
Through his teaching, personal guidance, and the institutions he has established, His Holiness guides individuals and communities seeking well-being, purpose, and inner peace.

Life

Swami Ramakrishnacharya’s life itself is a testament to yogic profundity. From a young age he achieved deep Samadhi, the highest state of yogic absorption. At the request of students and devotees, he has publicly demonstrated Bhu Samadhi (underground burial in meditation) on multiple occasions, astonishing onlookers by remaining in meditation beneath the earth for days. These demonstrations have kindled widespread curiosity and faith in the power of authentic Vedic yoga. Such samadhis illustrate the yogic teaching that at the pinnacle of spiritual practice, the soul can separate from bodily limitations and unite with the Divine at will – a feat extremely rare in modern times. Mahayogi Siddhababa is counted among the very few living saints capable of entering and sustaining nirvikalpa samadhi (complete absorption) even under observation, which is why devotees revere him as a “Siddha Purusha” (attained being). 

In 2019, an assembly of spiritual leaders from all major Hindu monastic lineages (Vaishnava, Shaiva, Shakta, Smarta, etc.) honored him with the title Jagadguru (“worldceptor” or universal teacher), formally recognizing him as a successor of Ramanandacharya and a Guru capable of guiding the entire world. This title was bestowed highlighting his extraordinary spiritual attainments, particularly his perfection in samādhi and ability to transmit that state to others. 

Under the guidance of his own Guru (Param Pujya Sri Narayan Das Ji Maharaj of Nasik), he revived Surat-Shabd Yoga practice and formulated it as “Himalayan Siddha Mahayog” for the benefit of sincere seekers worldwide. This sacred knowledge, known only to few ascetics in Himalayan caves, has now been made accessible to the public through his efforts. His Holiness initiates seekers through Shaktipāt Dīkṣā, the ancient method of transmitting spiritual energy (śakti) from Guru to disciple. He is a Shaktipat Acharya in the truest sense, capable of awakening a person’s Kundalini Shakti by a mere glance, touch, mantra, or even pure intention at a distance. This infusion of divine energy at initiation is the hallmark of Himalayan Siddha Mahayog, distinguishing it from practices that rely solely on the aspirant’s effort. Under Jagadguru Siddhababa’s guidance, thousands of people have received Kundalini awakening and tangible spiritual experiences, validating that the grace of a living master can spark rapid inner transformation.

Teachings
His Holiness emphasizes three foundational principles for joyful and meaningful living:
Seva · Sumiran · Samarpan
Selfless service, continuous inner remembrance, and wholehearted surrender to the source of creation.
At the heart of his guidance is the practice of Himalayan Siddha Mahayog Meditation, an integrated system for fostering mental and physical well-being and inner spiritual development.
Mahayogi Siddhababa’s teachings are intended for people from all walks of life. Rather than encouraging withdrawal from the world, they emphasize living with awareness, where spiritual practice strengthens one’s capacity to engage fully and responsibly with family, work and society alongside the journey to self-realization.
Key Teachings:
Inner development through Mahayog Meditation, self-inquiry and introspection
Befriending the mind and refining character
Ethical living grounded in awareness and responsibility
Devotion and discernment
Seva (selfless service) as a natural expression of inner realization

Ashrams & Global Presence
Under his guidance, ashrams and meditation centers have been established across Nepal and India, including Kathmandu, Pokhara, Surkhet, Tikapur, Chitwan, Devghat, Jaipur, and Nashik, as well as across North America, Europe and Australia.
These centers serve as spaces for practice, learning, service, and community, supporting reflection, education, and collective wellbeing.
The Academy’s head Ashram is Jagadguru Ramanandacharya Sewa Pith located in Barahachettra Dham, Chatara, Sunsari, Nepal. [Link to Ashram page]
