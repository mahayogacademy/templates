Kundalini, the Chakras, and the Inner Science of Awakening
Siddhababa explains that in the Vedic Sanātana tradition, the human body is not merely a physical structure but a living field of consciousness, governed by subtle energies, channels, and centers of awareness. This inner science, preserved through yogic lineages, describes the presence of 72,000 nāḍīs (subtle channels) and tens of thousands of chakras through which prāṇa flows.
Among these, sixteen principal and highly sensitive chakras hold special significance. Their awakening and refinement correspond to what the tradition calls the Ṣoḍaśa Saṁskāras, the sixteen sacred refinements that shape human life from birth to liberation. For practical understanding, this vast system is often summarized through the seven primary chakras (Sapta Chakra), which guide the journey from instinctual existence to divine realization.
At the heart of this journey lies Kuṇḍalinī Śakti.

Kuṇḍalinī: The Power That Sustains Creation
Kuṇḍalinī is not a metaphor, nor a dormant imagination. It is the fundamental conscious power through which creation arises, is sustained, and dissolves. She is the same supreme energy known by many sacred names such as Bhagavatī, Devī, Sītā, Rādhā, Lakṣmī, Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī, called differently yet one in essence.
Kuṇḍalinī always exists in a state of awareness. She never sleeps. What is described as “sleep” in yogic language refers only to her downward-facing orientation, which propels the cycle of repeated birth and death.
Punarapi jananam, punarapi maraṇam
Again and again birth, again and again death.
When her orientation turns upward through grace, purity, devotion, and inner discipline, the same force becomes the cause of awakening, knowledge, and liberation.
Mūlādhāra Chakra: The Root of Life and Death
Among the seven principal chakras, the Mūlādhāra Chakra is the foundation. Located at the center between the organs of excretion, it is the root support of life itself.
The word mūla means root, the source from which all growth arises. Just as a tree draws nourishment from its roots, all experiences of birth, death, action (karma), survival, and embodiment arise from this center. Hence it is called Mūlādhāra, the foundation of existence.
This chakra is:
square in form
yellow in color
associated with the earth element
empowered by the Laṁ bīja mantra
At its center resides the Svayambhū Liṅga, around which Kuṇḍalinī is coiled three and a half times, facing downward. In this state, life is driven by instinct, fear, desire, and survival.
The Mūlādhāra Chakra carries the life-sustaining force. Even at death, this chakra does not perish. Only the chakras of the physical sheath dissolve. The chakras themselves belong to the subtle body, which journeys onward with the soul.
As long as Kuṇḍalinī remains downward-facing, the individual remains bound to ignorance and confusion, pleasure and pain, gain and loss, and repeated birth and death.
Kuṇḍalinī Awakening: The Turning Point
When Kuṇḍalinī turns upward through Guru’s grace, pure conduct, pure thought, love, and sincere spiritual practice, this reversal is called Kuṇḍalinī Jāgaraṇa, awakening.
At that moment, the human being naturally inclines toward truth and higher knowledge.
Lead me from untruth to truth,
from darkness to light,
from death to immortality.
Thoughts become purified. Compassion, joy, wisdom, love, and clarity begin to arise. The ascent then continues through the higher chakras.
Svādhiṣṭhāna Chakra: The Realm of Sensitivity and Taste
Located below the navel, the Svādhiṣṭhāna Chakra governs the water element and is extremely sensitive. Although the tongue is considered the organ of taste, yogic science explains that taste is actually perceived through this chakra, not the tongue itself.
Here, emotions, desires, and creative impulses are refined. When purified, this chakra becomes a gateway from instinctual craving to conscious experience.
Maṇipūra Chakra: Fire, Vitality, and Mastery
Situated at the navel, the Maṇipūra Chakra is the center of the fire element and personal power.
When awakened, vitality increases, digestion and metabolism become balanced, and the body becomes strong, radiant, and attractive. One gains clarity, courage, and longevity.
A balanced Maṇipūra Chakra protects the body from many diseases and supports both physical strength and inner confidence.
Anāhata Chakra: The Seat of the Soul
The Anāhata Chakra, located at the center of the chest, is the realm of the air element and the dwelling place of the jīvātman, the individual soul.
When this chakra is purified, compassion becomes effortless, devotion deepens, the heart expands beyond ego, and direct experience of the Divine becomes possible.
A person with an awakened Anāhata Chakra embodies strength with gentleness and power with humility, firm like Hanumān yet tender in compassion.
Viśuddha Chakra: Purity, Knowledge, and Expression
Located in the throat, the Viśuddha Chakra is the domain of the ether element and Śiva-tattva.
Its awakening bestows clarity of speech, profound listening, poetic and scholarly brilliance, and aspiration for liberation.
Yogic texts describe that one who pierces this chakra gains subtle perception, refined hearing, and the ability to comprehend all forms of expression.
Ājñā Chakra: Command and Inner Vision
The Ājñā Chakra, located between the eyebrows, is the center of direct perception and command. When awakened, one realizes Oṁ, gains true knowledge of reality, and rises beyond duality.
Here, the seeker no longer follows instinct or emotion, but inner truth.
Sahasrāra Chakra: Fulfillment of Human Birth
The Sahasrāra Chakra, the thousand-petaled lotus at the crown of the head, is the culmination of the yogic journey.
When it awakens, the jīva becomes Śiva and the human becomes Nārāyaṇa. The purpose of birth is fulfilled.
Such a being abides in Self-realization and Brahman-realization, beyond time, fear, and death. This is the state of the liberated soul.

A Living, Experiential Science
This knowledge is not theoretical or imagined. It arises from direct yogic experience, preserved through traditional lineages. The teachings of Kuṇḍalinī and the chakras are precise, experiential, and scientific within their own domain.
They are not meant for curiosity alone, but for inner transformation.
Those who walk this path with sincerity, discipline, and grace do not merely learn about awakening. They become awakened.
